      Open interest & FII positioning

      Who is positioned, and are they adding or exiting?

      Price ↑ OI ↑Long build-upFresh longs. Trend has real conviction.
      Price ↓ OI ↑Short build-upFresh shorts. The fall is being backed.
      Price ↑ OI ↓Short coveringShorts running. Can reverse once covering ends.
      Price ↓ OI ↓Long unwindingLongs exiting. Often a healthier dip.

      OI never moves alone. Read it against price; the combination is the signal.

      3

      Option chain

      Where exactly are writers defending strikes?

      Open interest by strike

      Bars: red = call writers (ceiling) · green = put writers (floor)

      Highlighted in red: the change is bearish (call writers adding, or put writers leaving) Highlighted in green: the change is bullish (put writers adding, or call writers leaving) Significant = OI changed by 30% or more, and the change is at least 10% of the biggest OI in the chain. Outlined = 75% or more. IV under each strike = how far that strike's IV moved from what the index move alone predicts. Negative = selling pressure (writers), positive = buying pressure. This is what separates writing from buying. Faded bars are in the money. Changes there are reactions to losses or profits, so they count for less than changes in front of the price.

      4

      Put-call ratio

      Is sentiment stretched, and is that a signal or a trap?

      PCR is a contrarian gauge at the extremes. Very high means everyone is already bullish (trap risk); very low means bears are crowded. In between, direction matters: a sliding PCR means bulls are giving up.

      Inputs

      End-of-day PCR across all Nifty expiries, from NSE bhavcopy.

      PCR = put OI ÷ call OI (the standard convention; the slide had it inverted). Higher means more put writing, so bulls are stronger.

      5

      Implied volatility

      Is premium cheap or expensive right now?

      Rising volatility makes premiums expensive; falling volatility makes them cheap. IV at the low end of its range favours buying options, at the high end selling them. When IV keeps falling, bought premiums barely move; a sudden spike can hurt sold premiums.

      Inputs

      Live mode uses India VIX as Nifty's IV. Percentile and range come from the last 250 sessions.

      IV percentile = share of the last year's sessions when IV was below today's level.

      ⚑ Changes to watch

      Checked every 15 minutes · your Mac also shows a notification for each new flag

      Flags: red = a change that is bad for Indian stocks, amber = a big change worth watching. An indicator is flagged when it moves more than 2.5× its normal daily amount, hits a new 1-year high or low, or turns bad for India; the US 10-year is also flagged on a 0.15-point move in a week. The Fed is flagged when it changes rates, when 6-month Treasury bills sit more than 0.15% above or below the Fed rate (traders expect a hike or a cut), when that gap shifts 0.10% in a week, and in the 7 days before a decision.

      Headwind / tailwind is judged on the last month's move: crude ±5%, gold ±5%, dollar index ±2%, USD/INR ±1%, US 10-year ±0.20 pts, India 10-year ±0.15 pts, S&P 500 ±3%; US VIX above 25 is a headwind, below 15 a tailwind. The 1-year percentile shows where today's level sits in the past year (100 = highest). Source: CNBC market data, New York Fed.
